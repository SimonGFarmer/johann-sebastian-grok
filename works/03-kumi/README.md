# KUMI
### Toccata for Taiko Ensemble and Orchestra — Johann Sebastian Grok

One movement in five sections, played without a break · D minor to D major · 4′40″ · 143 bars

## Program note

*Kumi* means a group, and also a braid. Taiko players use the word for an ensemble that has learned to strike as one body. I wanted to write a taiko performance with an orchestra standing around it, not an orchestra with drums added.

It begins with one drum and one thread. The O-daiko speaks alone, and then a dulcimer answers with a spiral arpeggio: root, fifth, ninth, tenth, climbing like smoke. Every other line in the piece grows from that figure. In **Gathering**, the drums call and answer each other (*kakeai*). The strings pick the spiral up in close and open positions, and the bass walks down an old lament line from D. **The Storm** is the full *kumi-daiko*. Every eight bars, the music is thrown up a semitone (D, E♭, E, F, F♯ minor), and each time the drummers shout the change. The horns turn the spiral into a melody, and then the men's voices take it. The last pivot lands on D major and stops dead.

**The Eye** is the quiet inside the storm. The drums fall silent, the spiral turns slowly in a high, glassy string sound, and women's voices hold *oo* above one long, high violin note. A shout breaks it. **Return** brings everyone back to one rhythm, 3+3+2, which the drums, arpeggios and brass all share. The theme comes back on trumpets and full choir, and the hammer blows lead into an *oroshi*, the long roll that gets faster and faster. The piece ends on D major, one last DON, and a cry.

The orchestra may argue with the drum. The drum always wins.

— J. S. G.

## Material

- **The spiral** (Ignition, b3): an open arpeggio of root, fifth, ninth and tenth, `d4 a4 e5 f5`, climbing on to the twelfth and beyond. Every arpeggio in the piece walks its chord this way, open (first violins, dulcimers) or in close position (second violins, violas, lutes).
- **The cycle** (Gathering and Return): Dm · Dm7/C · B♭maj7 · B♭6 · Gm · Gm7/F · Em7♭5 · A7, over a lament bass falling from D to A.
- **The storm phrase**: eight bars, i · i6 · ♭VImaj7 · ♭VII · i · iv · ♭VIsus4 · ♭VI7. The last chord is the dominant of the key a semitone higher, so the phrase climbs D → E♭ → E → F → F♯ minor; the final ♭VI is D major itself.
- **The theme**: the spiral sung as a melody, `d a | g. f | f d | e | f a | b♭. a | b♭ e♭ | d a♭ f`, first in the horns, then the men's voices, then trumpets an octave higher with full choir.
- **The lock**: the Return's single rhythm, 3+3+2 twice in a bar (accents on sixteenths 1, 4, 7, 9, 12, 15), shared by every drum, every arpeggio and every brass stab.

Everything is at concert pitch.

**Reading the taiko staves.** The five taiko parts are unpitched. Each is written on a treble staff with placeholder notes, one note per kind of stroke:

| Note | Stroke |
|---|---|
| C4 | DON: a full stroke in the centre of the head (accents mark the big unison hits) |
| D4 | A soft or off-centre stroke (do, ko, tsu), or a left-hand ghost stroke |
| F4 | Flam (doDON: two sticks almost together; O-daiko only) |
| G4 | KA: a click on the rim or shell |
| A4 | Damped, or choke |

On the atarigane, C4 is CHAN (open) and D4 is CHIKI (damped). Rolls are written out stroke by stroke, so an *oroshi* is notated as notes that get faster: eighths, then triplets, sixteenths and thirty-seconds.
